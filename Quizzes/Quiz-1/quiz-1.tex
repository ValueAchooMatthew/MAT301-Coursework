\begin{question}
	Consider the following permutations in $S_7$:

	\begin{align*}
		\sigma &= (13)(256) \\
		\tau &= (417)(235) \\
	\end{align*}

	What is the cycle type of $\sigma$ and $\tau$?
\end{question}

\begin{solution}
	We know that both permutations belong in $S_7$, and so there are $7$
	elements to be permuted. We also know that the cycle type of a
	permutation is a list representing the lengths of each of the
	permutation's cycles, placed in descending order. Using this, we can:

	\begin{step}
		Find the cycle type of $\sigma$.
	\end{step}

	$\sigma$ has two explicitly written cycles: $(13)$ and $(256)$ which are of lengths 2 and 3 respectively. Since there are only 5 elements expressed in the cycle notation of $\sigma$, that mean that the other 2 elements of $S_7$, namely 4 and 7, were omitted for being fixed points. Because fixed points evidently have cycle lengths of 1, this leads us to conclude that the cycle type of $\sigma$ must be given by:

	\begin{gather*}
		(3, 2, 1, 1)
	\end{gather*}

	\begin{step}
		Find the cycle type of $\tau$.
	\end{step}

	By similar logic, $\tau$ has two cycles of length 3 each given by the notation. Since that makes for 6 elements total, the unaccounted for element, 6, must be fixed. Hence, the cycle type of $\tau$ is:

	\begin{gather*}
		(3, 3, 1)
	\end{gather*}
\end{solution}

\end{question}
